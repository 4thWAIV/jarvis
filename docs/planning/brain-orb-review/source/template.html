Draw the brain from anatomical filaments

What is wrong now: a sine pattern chooses bright bands across a dense triangle mesh. Those bands are not brain anatomy, and the mesh can look like a painted surface.

1. Follow the folds

Use the downloaded model's connected surface and curvature to trace continuous strands along ridges and into grooves. Remove the artificial stripe mask.

2. Grow from the core

Branch outward from the centered core, then join those surface strands. Keep the enclosing orb and its depth. Do not overlay a separate brain image.

3. Light whole strands

Green illuminates many paths softly. White and yellow illuminate fewer, brighter paths. Red illuminates a small set of bright paths and the outer contour. Dim paths retain their state colour.

4. Prove the volume

Review front, rear and oblique views. Filaments must follow the same organ in every view, with depth occlusion and no flat slices. Keep the resting orientation fixed.

Next design step: build this as a separate anatomical-filament study. The current page changes the window only; it does not pretend the brain redraw is complete.

Shared pulse behaviour

Use one gentle eight-second rhythm for optional status pulses, with only ±4% intensity variation. Errors and red states stay steadily lit. Events move along their paths; they do not flash the screen.

Reduced motion gives a still scene and steady light. State changes blend over about two seconds. Repeated events must be combined rather than creating competing pulses.

This study demonstrates the policy. Applying it throughout the product requires updating the existing orb, transmission and status animations. Implementation scope and validation.
